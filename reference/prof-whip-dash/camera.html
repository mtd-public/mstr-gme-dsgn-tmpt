What changes with the tilt

        

          

            

              The boulders stop being invisible
              
                Today they sit behind the camera and roll into frame when they claim a lane — that's why the lane ribbon
                exists. Tilt overhead and you can see all three at rest, so the ribbon becomes a confirmation rather
                than the primary read. Worth keeping either way, but it stops carrying the game.
              
            

            
design

          

          

            

              Swipe axis vs. screen axis
              
                At a diagonal, "left" on screen is no longer straight along the lanes. Up to about 40° of side offset it
                still reads fine; past that, a swipe left starts feeling like it moves the runner diagonally.
              
            

            
controls

          

          

            

              You see further ahead
              
                A tall portrait frame wastes height on sky in the chase view. Tilting down fills the frame with track —
                more reaction time for vermin, and the coin runs read as whole lines rather than as receding dots.
              
            

            
legibility

          

          

            

              The runner shrinks
              
                Overhead, the Professor reads as a hat and shoulders. If you want the fedora to stay the silhouette,
                keep height under about 9m or pull the FOV in to compensate.
              
            

            
character

          

          

            

              Shadows do more work
              
                The key light is already behind the runner. Tilted down, the boulders' cast shadows stretch across the
                lanes in full view — the single best thing the new angle buys you.
