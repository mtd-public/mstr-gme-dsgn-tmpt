Concept 01 — the core problem

The chase you can't see

A chase camera looks forward; the boulders are behind. If the player can't read which lanes are claimed, every hit feels unfair. Four devices put the rear threat on screen without ever turning the camera around.

  1. The camera does it

    This was a three-pip lane ribbon back when the camera sat behind the runner and the blocks lived outside the frame. The overhead diagonal keeps every lane and every boulder on screen, so the ribbon was confirming what the player could already see — it is gone, and the two bars it shared space with grew into its place.

  2. Shadows arriving first

    The key light sits behind the runner, so each boulder throws a long shadow past him and onto the slabs ahead. Shadow width reads as distance: a hard black bar creeping up your lane is one second out.

  3. Grit at the frame edge

    Limestone dust, tinted red and tightened as the boulder in your lane closes. Peripheral, never blocking.

  4. Boulders roll into frame

    Idle boulders sit 11.5m back — behind the camera, out of shot. When a lane is claimed the block sweeps past the lens into view. You don't read a distance; you watch it arrive.

Concept 02 — the reason to go there

Gold where it hurts

A lane that only hurts is a lane you never enter, and the boulders stop mattering. So 70% of coin runs are deliberately threaded through the lane a boulder owns or is about to, and the payout scales with how much trouble you're in. The whole game is one question: how long can you stay in the red lane?

Concept 04 — the world

One 12-metre sacbé

The causeway is a single 12m prefab recycled six at a time: three limestone lanes, a painted glyph band at every joint, serpent balustrades, stelae, braziers, jungle pressing in. Seeded variation keeps each module deterministic but never twinned with its neighbour.

Module · 12 m

Temple causeway

Lane slabs alternate two limestone tones every 2.86m and a cinnabar glyph band crosses each joint — that banding is what gives the speed read at 20 m/s that a flat floor loses. Everything above 3m is decoration and gets culled first on low-end devices.

Concept 06 — the hand

One thumb, portrait, no buttons

On a phone the whole control surface is the screen. Nothing interactive sits in the bottom 12% — that is where the thumb rests and where the home indicator lives.

  1. Swipe ← / → : switch lane

    Fires the moment the swipe passes 28px rather than on release — at 20 m/s, waiting for pointerup is a hit. The origin resets after each one, so a long drag crosses two lanes.

  2. Tap : crack the whip

    Anywhere, 0.30s cooldown, no aim. It reaches your own lane only, which keeps the decision about when.

  3. Haptics carry the grind

    A short tick per HP segment lost, escalating. On a tablet without haptics the frame edge pulses instead.

  4. Landscape is the same game

    Tablet and desktop get the identical 9:16 column, pillarboxed, with the stats card and the ◀ / whip / ▶ footer. The lanes never widen.
